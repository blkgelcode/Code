# SEO & Discoverability Guide

Writing titles, descriptions, and hashtags for three brands. Optimize for
discovery, but the copy still has to sound like Omnia — not a generic SEO
template.

## Voice rules (non-negotiable, all copy)

- Concrete and countable over vague. "3 workouts this week," not "getting
  healthier." If a specific number/detail is genuinely hers to supply and
  unavailable, leave a flagged blank — don't invent a placeholder stat.
- No filler, no generic creator-speak ("you won't believe," "this changed
  my life," "must watch"). If a line could be pasted under anyone's video,
  cut it.
- Comfortable with real, specific personal detail — don't sanitize it for
  "broad appeal."
- Flag it rather than resolve it if a description/hashtag choice would
  contradict something already locked (a title convention, a CTA, a series
  name) — don't quietly pick a side.

## Workflow

1. Confirm which brand/series and platform (YouTube, TikTok, IG) — pillars,
   title conventions, and hashtag tiers differ by both.
2. Pull the real specifics of the episode/video (transcript, notes, or what
   she tells you) before writing — don't draft generic copy and fill in
   details after.
3. Draft title + description + hashtag set together so they don't repeat
   the same phrase three times.
4. Flag: any naming/CTA conflict with what's locked, and any spot where you
   don't have the specific detail needed and would otherwise have to guess.

## Titles

- Front-load the searchable term (location, topic, series name) — don't
  bury it behind a clever phrase the algorithm can't parse.
- YouTube titles can carry more context than TikTok/IG — use the extra room
  for the specific hook, not filler words.
- Match existing naming conventions exactly (e.g. "Young & Outside in
  Tokyo") — check the current series name against the brand files below
  before writing it; several have been renamed more than once.
- **@blkgrlco HHH Run (2026-11-13):** the specific story comes first and
  the day number is secondary. "Day N of 90" as a title format is retired;
  put "Day [verified]/90" after the specific idea or in a small on-screen
  label, and verify every day number against the calendar.
- Don't duplicate the exact same phrase across title and thumbnail — split
  the information so each asset earns its own real estate (e.g. thumbnail:
  "NOT A VISION BOARD" → title: "Hot. Healthy. Happy. My Actual 90-Day
  Plan").

## Descriptions

- Open with 1–2 sentences stating what the video actually is, using core
  keyword terms naturally — this is what search snippets pull.
- Follow with real specifics from the episode (what happened, where, what
  changed) — not a recap that could apply to any episode in the series.
- No forced CTA on @blkgrlco (the Pin Board CTA was retired 2026-11-11);
  include a brand's locked CTA only where one still exists.
- End with the series' sign-off line where the format calls for it.
- Keep it in her voice — short, direct, no corporate transition phrases
  ("in this video, we will be discussing").

## Hashtags

The three tiers are a menu, not a quota (2026-11-13: add only genuinely
relevant hashtags, if useful; never stuff keywords). When hashtags are
used, mix across tiers rather than stacking one:
1. **Broad/discovery** — platform-wide reach terms for the content type.
2. **Niche/community** — terms the actual target audience searches.
3. **Brand/series-specific** — matched to whichever brand the post belongs
   to.

Never reuse a retired series hashtag on new posts (see the retired-names
list below).

## Platform packaging (@blkgrlco HHH Run, 2026-11-13)

Her Platform Packaging Guide (verbatim:
`../brand-bible/source/blkgrlco-platform-packaging-guide.md`; research
checked 2026-11-12). The principle: **one truthful core story, presented
for the surface where people meet it.** Not three identities, not three
elaborate edits per episode. Reuse the core edit; change only what has a
clear platform-specific purpose. Every example below is proposed
packaging, usable only when the footage supports the words.

- **YouTube long-form** (only when she actually makes one; never generate
  it automatically): specific story before the episode label in the
  title; one readable idea on the thumbnail with a relevant image, not a
  collage of the week; the opening states the week's real question or
  lesson, no long greeting, calendar animation or series explainer first;
  the description explains the episode in plain language, then series
  context and genuinely related videos; end-screen points to a related
  episode, not an unrelated upload.
- **YouTube Shorts:** name the task or tension in the title; first frame
  is the relevant action or her face starting the story, never a static
  cover inserted as an intro; spoken opening is the real situation; pick
  a representative thumbnail frame (set in the YouTube app, not Studio)
  rather than commissioning a long-form-style design; resolve the opening
  question, keep the honest result, end without padding.
- **TikTok:** choose the intent first (relatable moment, or an answer to
  a specific search question; a diary never poses as a tutorial);
  conversational opening in the actual situation; on-screen label of the
  real topic; one natural caption sentence plus only relevant hashtags;
  short cover label for a coherent profile (an organizational choice,
  not a disclosed ranking factor); trend or audio only if it fits her
  voice and the story. Mark a search phrase **unvalidated** unless
  Creator Search Insights or real data supports it ("Sunday reset",
  "meal prep for one", "evening skincare routine" are candidates, not
  verified demand). Never force "for one", "beginner" or an ingredient
  into the package unless it describes the episode.
- **Instagram Reels:** lead with a recognizable feeling or useful action;
  clear topic label on the cover plus restrained HHH branding; caption
  starts with the specific topic, then the personal meaning; genuine
  save/share value, no engagement bait and no required "send this" CTA;
  Stories are optional, never a per-post requirement; keywords and
  hashtags go in the caption, not the comments; export a clean master
  with readable captions, correct framing and **no other platform's
  visible watermark** (Instagram says low-resolution or watermarked
  Reels may be shown less).
- **Eligibility is separate from packaging:** low views alone never prove
  suppression; Instagram's Account Status is where a recommendation
  restriction would show.

**For each requested platform, return** (her checklist, 2026-11-13):
title or caption; cover text, thumbnail concept or selected frame;
opening line and on-screen text; intended viewer and story promise; one
test hypothesis and the metrics to review. Never publish, schedule or
overwrite originals without authorization. The checklist itself is §7 of
`../agents/EDITOR_CHECKLIST.md`. Generate only the platforms
asked for. Avoid unsupported algorithm weights, universal runtime or CTR
targets, hashtag quotas, compulsory trends, and any claim that one weak
post proves suppression.

## Per-brand keyword pillars, CTAs, and hashtags

- Core terms: hot healthy happy, glow up, confidence, beauty, fitness,
  nutrition, self-improvement, accountability.
- Series: Weekly Vlogs, 90-Day HHH Run, "Who Is She?"
- **Locked CTA (90-Day HHH Run):** "make your own Pin Board" — not older
  variants like "comment your own rule."

  beautymaxx").

  #hothealthyhappy.

  "travel guide" language.

  #stayugly (confirmed 2026-10-03).

## How to talk to her

- Short and direct, no preamble.
- Given a one-line correction, apply it exactly and move on — don't
  re-explain the change back.

# Analytics System

## Read this first: what analytics are *for* here

The studio-level goal is consistency, storytelling craft, and putting out
content she loves — not subs, watch-hours, or monetization (see
`../brand-bible/BRAND_BIBLE.md` §9; a monetization target was set and
scrapped the same day in 2026-09 for this reason). Analytics in this system
exist to answer **"is the craft improving and is this sustainable to keep
making,"** not "is this growing fast enough." Don't reintroduce
growth-chasing framing into reports or recommendations.

## What gets tracked (once there's real data)

Views, impressions, CTR, average view duration, retention curve, watch
time, engagement (likes/comments/shares/saves), followers/subscribers
gained, traffic sources, search performance, returning-viewer rate,
demographics where available.

## How conclusions get made

For every review: **What worked → What didn't → Why → What to change → What
to try next** — framed as craft feedback, not a growth mandate.

Rules:
- No conclusions from insufficient data — state the sample size/timeframe
  behind any claim. (4 videos and 28 minutes of total watch time is not
  enough to conclude much yet — say so plainly rather than overreading it.)
- Correlation is not causation.
- One variable at a time in a controlled experiment, unless there's a
  stated strategic reason to change more than one.

## Her story and growth rules (2026-11-13)

From her Story and Growth Checklist (verbatim:
`../brand-bible/source/blkgrlco-story-and-growth-checklist.md`), the
three that govern every analytics read here:

- Use actual audience feedback to suggest **one specific experiment at
  a time**. Distinguish observations from assumptions.
- **Do not overhaul the strategy after one low-performing post**, and
  never treat low views as a judgment of her personality. Look for
  patterns across comparable posts.
- Keep production manageable: no extra posts, footage requirements or
  complicated edits without a clear purpose. A recommendation that adds
  work needs to say what it's for.

## HHH weekly edit test (@blkgrlco, from 2026-11-11)

Her HHH Weekly Edit Breakdown (verbatim in
`../brand-bible/source/blkgrlco-hhh-weekly-edit-breakdown.md`) sets up a
two-week test of seven repeatable story types. How to read it, in her
own framing:

- Repeat each story type when it fits the real week. Record results
  seven days after each approved publication.
- Compare matching topics, similar lengths, and the same platform. One
  previous Reel is a reference, not a universal baseline.
- **Opening:** early-retention or chose-to-watch/skip measures, without
  treating differently defined platform metrics as interchangeable
  (Instagram's "average watch time" is watch time ÷ initial views, not a
  completion rate; YouTube Shorts report chose-to-view, average view
  duration and average percentage viewed).
- **Story progression:** where viewers leave relative to the setup,
  attempt and payoff. The Bin's retention loop (Plan → Edit & retention)
  answers this for YouTube uploads with a clip order.
- **Depth:** average watch time read alongside runtime and the platform's
  percentage viewed where available.
- **Connection:** substantive comments, shares, saves and attributed
  follows where the platform exposes them.
- **Decision:** repeat an approach that improves across more than one
  post; mixed results mean test again, not rebuild the channel.

The seven-day checkpoint and the two-week window are practical choices,
not platform rules or a controlled experiment. YouTube's own guidance:
compare stronger and weaker Shorts on watch time, likes and comments
rather than against a viral hit; it does not favor a particular Shorts
format or require a posting cadence. This stays craft feedback per §9 of
the Brand Bible.

## Experiment types

Hooks, titles, thumbnails, video length, editing style, topics, posting
times, CTAs, content formats — framed as "what does she enjoy making and
does the audience engage with it," not as growth levers. Retention danger
zone to watch: ~75% mark (see `growth/CONTENT_STRATEGY.md` packaging
logic) — that's where a texture/cutaway beat belongs.

Each concluded experiment gets logged in `../brand-bible/CREATIVE_MEMORY.md`
under "Successful experiments."

# BLKGRLCO: Platform Packaging Guide

Research checked November 12, 2026. This guide covers organic discovery for your 90-day Hot, Happy, Healthy challenge, not paid-ad targeting.

The recommendation: keep one truthful core story, then tailor its presentation to the surface where people encounter it. Do not create three unrelated identities or three elaborate edits of every episode.

## Audience and scope

Use this working audience hypothesis: people trying to build realistic self-care and wellness habits while living an ordinary life. Lead with a specific situation they can recognize, not an assumed demographic or an unverified audience profile.

Your series question remains: “Can I become hot, happy, and healthy in 90 days?” The day number provides continuity; the individual episode still needs to make sense to someone meeting you for the first time.

This research did not inspect your current private account analytics, keyword demand, or recommendation eligibility. Examples below are proposed packaging, not proven winners; use only language supported by your actual footage and experience.

## What the platforms actually disclose

| Surface | Supported guidance | Recommended packaging response |
|---|---|---|
| **YouTube Home, Suggested, and long-form Search** | Home considers relevance and satisfaction for similar viewers plus viewing history; Search considers title, description, and video relevance to the query and engagement for that search ([YouTube FAQ](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/141805?hl=en)). Titles and thumbnails should set expectations that the opening delivers ([YouTube performance guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16559650)). | Package a specific question, useful outcome, or honest experiment. For a searchable topic, name the task naturally; for Home/Suggested, make the situation compelling without assuming existing series knowledge. |
| **YouTube Shorts** | Ranking includes whether viewers choose to watch, average view duration, average percentage viewed, likes, and post-watch surveys; Search also considers metadata relevance and whether viewers click and watch ([YouTube Shorts discovery](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/11914225?hl=en&co=YOUTUBE._YTVideoType=shorts)). | Design the opening for someone already scrolling a video feed. Also provide an understandable title and representative thumbnail frame for other discovery contexts. |
| **TikTok For You and Search** | For You considers interactions such as full watches/skips, likes, shares, comments, and watch time; interactions generally carry more weight for most users, while Search generally places more weight on content information, including query match ([TikTok recommendations](https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/exploring-videos/how-tiktok-recommends-content)). | Choose a relatable situation for For You or a concrete question/task for Search. Use a clear opening and natural topic language, rather than chasing unrelated trends or stuffing keywords. |
| **Instagram Reels, Explore, Feed, and Stories** | Instagram uses different ranking systems across surfaces; its Reels explanation describes predictions such as resharing, watching through, and liking, while Explore emphasizes predicted likes, saves, and shares ([Instagram ranking explanation](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/instagram-ranking-explained)). Its creator FAQ describes engagement per viewer and the likelihood of watching and engaging ([Instagram creator FAQ](https://creators.instagram.com/faq)). | Give a new viewer a clear reason to watch and a genuine reason to relate, save, or share. Treat a readable cover as navigation and presentation, not a substitute for an effective opening. |

These are disclosed signals, not a complete algorithm or a fixed weighting formula. The sources do not establish a universal winning runtime, view threshold, or guaranteed number of impressions.

## YouTube: package the promise

### Long-form, when you actually make a longer video

- **Title:** Put the specific story before the episode label. Example: “What Helped Me Stay Consistent This Week | Hot, Happy, Healthy,” only if that is what the video shows.
- **Thumbnail:** Use one readable idea and a relevant image. My suggested concept is an actual reset moment with the text “WHAT WORKED?” rather than a collage of every weekly activity.
- **Opening:** Introduce the week's real question or lesson, then show why it matters. Avoid a long greeting, calendar animation, or full series explanation before the substance.
- **Description:** Explain the episode in plain language first, then add series context and genuinely relevant related videos.
- **Next step:** Offer a related episode or recap that continues the same interest, not an unrelated upload.

This follows YouTube's guidance on accurate packaging, relevance, and delivering on the title/thumbnail promise; the wording and design choices above are editorial suggestions ([YouTube FAQ](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/141805?hl=en), [performance guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16559650)).

### Shorts

- **Title:** Name the task or tension: “Meal Prep for Tomorrow When I’m Tired,” if true. Put “Day [verified]/90” after the specific idea or in a small on-screen label.
- **First frame:** Show the relevant action or your face beginning the actual story. Do not open on a static cover just to advertise the episode.
- **Spoken opening:** “I'm getting tomorrow's lunch ready before I run out of energy,” if supported by the recording.
- **Thumbnail frame:** Choose a clear, representative frame rather than commissioning a separate long-form-style design by default.
- **Ending:** Resolve the opening question and preserve the honest result. End without padding.

YouTube's current creation guide allows selecting a Shorts thumbnail frame in the YouTube app before or after upload; it says thumbnail editing is not available in Studio ([YouTube Shorts creation](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10343433?hl=en&co=GENIE.Platform=Android)). Its discovery guide lists no minimum posting cadence and says it does not favor a particular Shorts format ([YouTube Shorts discovery](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/11914225?hl=en&co=YOUTUBE._YTVideoType=shorts)).

## TikTok: make the situation or search intent obvious

- **Choose an intent:** Is this episode a relatable moment, or does it answer a specific search question? It can serve both, but do not force a personal diary to pose as a comprehensive tutorial.
- **Opening:** Use the actual situation in conversational language. Example: “I want tomorrow's lunch ready, but I don't want to spend tonight cooking.”
- **On-screen text:** Label the real topic, such as “Meal prep for tomorrow.” This is a clarity recommendation, not a claim that text placement has a disclosed ranking weight.
- **Caption:** Write one natural sentence explaining what happens, then add only genuinely relevant hashtags if useful. Do not promise outcomes the video does not deliver.
- **Cover:** Use a short topic label for a coherent profile, such as “MEAL PREP NIGHT.” This is an organizational choice, not a documented For You ranking factor.
- **Trend/audio:** Use it only if it fits your voice and the story. Do not replace a relevant episode with an unrelated trend merely to post more.

TikTok explicitly identifies query relevance, hashtags, and sounds among the information used by its systems, but its ranking explainer does not disclose a cover-design weight or a keyword-repetition formula ([TikTok recommendations](https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/exploring-videos/how-tiktok-recommends-content)).

### Research topics before calling them “search optimized”

TikTok's Creator Search Insights shows searched topics, content gaps, related searches, and search-performance analytics; the “Searches by followers” filter requires more than 1,000 followers ([Creator Search Insights](https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/growing-your-audience/creator-search-insights)). Use it to validate candidate topics such as “Sunday reset,” “meal prep for one,” or “evening skincare routine”; those phrases are research candidates here, not verified high-demand terms.

Do not force “for one,” “beginner,” or a specific ingredient into the package unless it accurately describes your episode. If search data cannot be accessed, label the proposed phrase unvalidated.

## Instagram: package recognition and connection

- **Opening:** Lead with a recognizable feeling or useful action. Example: “Doing one thing tonight so tomorrow feels less rushed,” if that reflects your experience.
- **Cover:** Give the episode a clear topic label plus restrained series branding. Suggested cover: “TOMORROW’S LUNCH,” with a smaller HHH label.
- **Caption:** Start with the specific topic and add the personal meaning. Example: “Meal-prep night for tomorrow's lunch. Tonight's goal was getting it ready, not making it elaborate.”
- **Share/save value:** Include a real takeaway worth remembering or passing along. Do not manufacture engagement bait or require a “send this” CTA.
- **Stories, optionally:** Use a simple check-in or response prompt when you want conversation. Do not add a Story requirement to every post.
- **Export:** Use a clean master, readable captions, appropriate framing, and no other platform's visible watermark.

Instagram's creator guidance recommends relevant keywords and hashtags in the caption rather than comments, and its ranking explanation says low-resolution or watermarked Reels may be made less visible ([Instagram recommendations guidance](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/instagram-recommendations-eligibility-tips-creators?locale=hu_HU), [ranking explanation](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/instagram-ranking-explained)). Meta's January 2026 update also reports increased prevalence of original content in US Instagram recommendations, but does not provide a creator-specific success formula ([Meta update](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/01/2026-ai-drives-performance/?utm)).

Eligibility is separate from packaging: Instagram recommends checking Account Status for potential recommendation restrictions rather than guessing from low views alone ([Instagram recommendations guidance](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/instagram-recommendations-eligibility-tips-creators?locale=hu_HU)). This guide does not establish that your account has any restriction.

## What to measure

Use the following as a practical review plan, not a list of equally weighted algorithm inputs. Compare similar topics and runtimes on the same platform and at the same post age.

- **YouTube long-form:** Review impressions and CTR by traffic source where available, then opening retention, watch time, and whether the delivered story matches the package. Do not optimize clicks while ignoring disappointed viewers; YouTube describes appeal, engagement, and satisfaction as separate performance dimensions ([YouTube performance guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16559650)).
- **YouTube Shorts:** Review chose-to-view versus swiped-away, average view duration, average percentage viewed, and meaningful audience response. These viewing signals are explicitly discussed in YouTube's Shorts guidance ([YouTube Shorts discovery](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/11914225?hl=en&co=YOUTUBE._YTVideoType=shorts)).
- **TikTok:** Review available watch-time, retention/completion, shares, and follow outcomes; use Creator Search Insights to review search performance separately. Search analytics supports 7-day, 14-day, and custom ranges ([Creator Search Insights](https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/growing-your-audience/creator-search-insights)).
- **Instagram:** Review available watch-time, skip/retention, share/save, non-follower distribution, and attributed-follow information. Views include plays and replays, while average watch time is watch time divided by initial views, so neither should be casually interpreted as unique people or a completion rate ([Instagram Reel insights](https://help.instagram.com/202865988324236/)).

My suggested first test is two weeks of the existing weekly plan, with a seven-day checkpoint for each post. Keep the core edit stable and test one packaging element within each platform, such as the opening angle, rather than changing duration, topic, music, and captions simultaneously.

Cross-platform performance is not a controlled A/B test because audiences, distribution, and measurement differ. Record unavailable metrics as unavailable, and treat one winner as a clue to repeat, not proof of causation.

## Evidence limits

Some official pages are undated or describe earlier versions of the platforms, so this is current-access research, not a claim that every cited page was published in 2026. Instagram's fetched FAQ also contains conflicting duration guidance, including 90-second and three-minute references; I have therefore not turned those passages into a universal length requirement ([Instagram creator FAQ](https://creators.instagram.com/faq)).

I did not verify a fixed TikTok “five-point” formula or a universal numerical Instagram ranking hierarchy from the official sources reviewed. Treat algorithm multipliers and guaranteed benchmarks as unverified unless a current authoritative source actually supplies them.

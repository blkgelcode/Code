# BLKGRLCO: HHH Weekly Edit Breakdown

Seven repeatable edits for your 90-day Hot, Happy, Healthy challenge: two skincare nights, two workouts, one meal-prep day, one therapy day, and Sunday recap/reset. This is an editorial plan for Claude Code, not a review of the footage in afv5.mov or a prescription for your personal routines.

## What the research supports

- **Immediate, accurate context:** YouTube recommends concise openings that deliver on the title/thumbnail promise, followed by storytelling that maintains interest ([YouTube performance guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16559650)).
- **Useful text and voiceover:** YouTube recommends on-screen text for clearer context and accessibility, and voiceover to add personality, reactions, and missing context ([YouTube Shorts editing tips](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13380879)).
- **One understandable story:** Creators featured by TikTok recommend starting with something tangible, using a clear hook, and adding captions; these are practitioner recommendations, not proven platform ranking rules ([TikTok creator guidance](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/scroll-stopping-tips-from-top-tiktok-creators?lang=en-GB)).
- **No magic format or posting quota:** YouTube says it does not favor a particular Shorts format and does not require a minimum posting cadence; its performance signals include chose-to-view, average view duration, average percentage viewed, and viewer enjoyment ([YouTube discovery guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/11914225?co=YOUTUBE._YTVideoType=shorts&hl=en)).
- **Clarity without losing personality:** Chloe Jenkinson's four-C framework emphasizes curiosity, cutting, clarity, and connection; it is a useful editing framework, not a guarantee of growth ([reference video](https://youtu.be/pA222K5UP14?is=YYu9fqy1bBpHATo3)).

The specific shot order, sample dialogue, and timing maps below are tailored creative recommendations. The sources do not establish an ideal duration for a skincare video, workout video, therapy reflection, or weekly recap.

## Your retention baseline

Your supplied screenshots show a 1:49 Reel with 14 seconds average watch time and a sharp early retention decline. They also show one follow, a 37.8% skip rate labeled “Lower,” and Instagram views above the displayed typical-Reel line.

The 348 displayed views comprise 59 Instagram views and 289 Facebook views; the views-over-time graph is Instagram-only. These are one Reel's snapshot results, not proof of a channel-wide problem or a specific cause.

Our test: make the premise clear earlier and remove repeated information while preserving honest reactions. Do not cut every video to 14 seconds or treat 14/109 as a completion rate; Instagram defines average watch time as watch time divided by initial views ([Instagram Insights definitions](https://help.instagram.com/202865988324236/)).

## Shared editing standard

- **Default format:** A vertical daily edit suitable for Reels, TikTok, and YouTube Shorts. Sunday is also a vertical recap/reset here; a longer YouTube recap is a separate deliverable, not an automatic extra.
- **Opening:** The actual situation or question first. Put “Day [verified number]/90” in a small context label instead of a long spoken introduction.
- **Story:** One goal → attempt → actual result → personal takeaway.
- **Text:** Readable speech captions and occasional useful labels, not a second competing paragraph. Verify names, numbers, and transcription.
- **Pacing:** Change shots when the information changes. No compulsory cut every two seconds, constant zooms, or effect quota.
- **Sound:** Keep speech intelligible, use relevant natural sound, and place cleared music beneath it. Emotional moments do not require dramatic music.
- **Ending:** Place the calendar tick or honest miss with the result. A short audience question is optional.
- **Truth:** All example lines are conditional. Use them only when they match the footage; never invent reluctance, improvement, a completed goal, or a lesson.

The timestamps below describe example output timelines, not source timecodes, mandatory durations, or deadlines for a payoff. Expand or shorten them to fit the actual story; do not add filler or truncate an important thought to hit a number.

## Skincare night A: the routine story

**Purpose:** Show the act of making time for your existing routine. This is about the habit and the experience, not a product demonstration or overnight treatment result.

**Possible opening:** “Tonight's goal is simple: finish my evening routine and get to bed.”

| Example output time | Edit |
|---|---|
| 0–3s | Start on an actual action or direct-to-camera line establishing tonight's goal. |
| 3–7s | Give one relevant detail about the situation. Add the verified day/goal label. |
| 7–22s | Use two or three different stages of the routine, with natural sound or concise commentary. |
| 22–30s | Show what was actually completed and your honest reaction. |
| 30–35s | Calendar tick/miss and a short closing thought, if available. |

- **Keep:** A funny aside, a genuine tired expression, or a moment that explains why the routine mattered tonight.
- **Cut:** Repeated product holding, every pump/application angle, long silent setup, or the full challenge introduction.
- **Payoff:** Whether you completed your intended routine, not a claim that your skin transformed.
- **Optional question:** “What's the last thing you do before bed?”
- **Research connection:** Apply concise setup and use captions to clarify the action rather than narrating every step ([YouTube guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16559650), [editing tips](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13380879)).

## Skincare night B: the personal check-in

**Purpose:** Use the familiar routine as the setting for one personal thought. This should not repeat night A's “here are my steps” edit.

**Possible opening:** “One thing I'm noticing about this challenge is [actual observation].”

| Example output time | Edit |
|---|---|
| 0–4s | Begin with the observation, not a product lineup. |
| 4–10s | Briefly explain the real situation behind it. |
| 10–27s | Continue the thought using one concrete example; support it with relevant routine footage. |
| 27–35s | State what you're keeping, changing, or still unsure about. |
| 35–40s | Close naturally; show the calendar result only if verified. |

- **Keep:** Your face, phrasing, humor, and a meaningful pause.
- **Cut:** A second unrelated topic, repeated background, or decorative B-roll that obscures the reflection.
- **Payoff:** A clearer understanding of your experience, not a forced inspirational lesson.
- **Optional question:** “What have you learned about your own routines lately?”
- **Boundary:** Never alter treatment frequency or technique for content. Avoid filters or before/after edits that imply an unverified treatment effect.
- **Research connection:** Use voiceover and retained personality to add context and connection, rather than replacing the creator with a generic montage ([YouTube editing tips](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13380879), [four-C reference](https://youtu.be/pA222K5UP14?is=YYu9fqy1bBpHATo3)).

## Workout A: the goal and attempt

**Purpose:** Follow one real objective from your planned session. It need not be a personal record or an increase in weight.

**Possible opening:** “Today's workout goal is [actual objective]. Here's how it went.”

| Example output time | Edit |
|---|---|
| 0–3s | State the objective over a relevant moment or to-camera clip. |
| 3–8s | Add the minimum context needed to understand it. |
| 8–25s | Show the attempt, one informative detail, and a genuine reaction. |
| 25–34s | Establish what happened, including a modified or unfinished goal. |
| 34–40s | Add your takeaway and verified calendar result. |

- **Keep:** A moment of effort, a helpful cue with permission, or an adjustment that explains the outcome.
- **Cut:** The whole commute, every warm-up/set, repeated exercise angles, or a long motivational preamble.
- **Payoff:** An answer to the actual opening objective.
- **Optional question:** “What made your workout worthwhile today?”
- **Boundary:** Don't alter the training session for filming or turn an isolated clip into exercise advice.
- **Research connection:** Match the opening promise to the delivered story and maintain clear progression ([YouTube performance guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16559650)).

## Workout B: the expectation and reality

**Purpose:** Reveal something different about the second session: an expectation, discovery, enjoyable moment, or difficulty. Do not manufacture a setback just to distinguish it from workout A.

**Possible opening:** “I expected [truth] from today's workout. What stood out was [truth].”

| Example output time | Edit |
|---|---|
| 0–4s | Introduce the real expectation or the moment that surprised you. |
| 4–10s | Explain just enough for a stranger to understand. |
| 10–26s | Show the relevant session footage and what actually happened. |
| 26–35s | Reflect on what it meant to you, without exaggerating. |
| 35–40s | Close with the verified outcome or the next appropriate adjustment. |

- **Keep:** Humor, surprise, enjoyment, or an honest comment that differs from the first workout.
- **Cut:** Another generic full-session montage or a claim of progress unsupported by the footage.
- **Payoff:** What you learned or experienced, not necessarily improved performance.
- **Fallback:** If nothing distinct happened, make a straightforward shorter update or fold the footage into Sunday.
- **Research connection:** A focused, tangible story is a practitioner-backed approach; emotional connection is an editorial choice, not an algorithm guarantee ([TikTok creator guidance](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/scroll-stopping-tips-from-top-tiktok-creators?lang=en-GB), [four-C reference](https://youtu.be/pA222K5UP14?is=YYu9fqy1bBpHATo3)).

## Meal-prep day: making the next days easier

**Purpose:** Show one preparation goal and its real outcome. Default to a personal story, not a complete recipe tutorial.

**Possible opening:** “I'm getting [meal] ready so tomorrow has one less thing to figure out.”

| Example output time | Edit |
|---|---|
| 0–3s | Establish what you're preparing and why. |
| 3–8s | Show the starting point and the relevant constraint, if there is one. |
| 8–28s | Choose three meaningful stages: preparation, cooking/assembly, and portioning. |
| 28–38s | Show the actual finished food and say what it covers. |
| 38–45s | Give an honest verdict and the calendar result. |

- **Keep:** Food sounds, one useful detail, a candid reaction, and clear evidence of the completed preparation.
- **Cut:** Every ingredient introduction, repetitive chopping, lengthy grocery footage, or multiple shots of the same container.
- **Payoff:** What you prepared. Claim it made tomorrow easier only after you have an actual follow-up.
- **Optional question:** “What's one meal you're glad you prepared ahead?”
- **Boundary:** If presenting a recipe rather than a diary, retain necessary quantities and safe instructions; don't label an incomplete montage a complete tutorial.
- **Research connection:** Text can provide context during fast edits; the edit should still deliver what its opening promised ([YouTube editing tips](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13380879), [performance guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16559650)).

## Therapy day: showing up without oversharing

**Purpose:** Document making room for care, if you want to share it. The session itself is not the content.

**Possible opening:** “Today's challenge goal is keeping an appointment I made for myself.”

| Example output time | Edit |
|---|---|
| 0–4s | A brief home check-in establishes the goal. |
| 4–10s | Show one neutral preparation moment, if available. |
| 10–22s | Use an optional home reflection afterward, limited to what you're comfortable sharing. |
| 22–30s | Acknowledge the actual outcome and close quietly. |

- **Keep:** Your chosen boundaries, natural tone, and an honest statement without a tidy emotional resolution.
- **Cut:** Private session material, appointment screens, clinic identifiers, other clients, or an edit that suggests instant healing.
- **Payoff:** Showing up, rescheduling, or acknowledging what actually happened. Do not assume attendance from the calendar.
- **Optional question:** “What's one way you're making time for yourself?” No question is required.
- **Fallback:** A short, privacy-safe check-in is enough; you can also choose not to post this day.
- **Research connection:** Apply connection and concise context without interpreting either as a requirement for emotional disclosure ([four-C reference](https://youtu.be/pA222K5UP14?is=YYu9fqy1bBpHATo3), [YouTube performance guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16559650)).

## Sunday: recap and reset

**Purpose:** Explain what the week taught you and show one manageable reset. Add reflection rather than simply replaying six daily videos.

**Possible opening:** “This week's biggest lesson wasn't [expectation]. It was [actual lesson].” If there is no clear lesson yet, use “Here's what worked this week and what I'm still figuring out.”

| Example output time | Edit |
|---|---|
| 0–5s | Open with the strongest real observation or turning point. |
| 5–12s | Establish the week/day range and brief challenge context. |
| 12–28s | Show one win and one difficulty, using short excerpts as evidence. |
| 28–40s | Add new reflection: how you actually felt about hot, happy, and healthy this week. |
| 40–54s | Show one reset task tied to a specific adjustment for next week. |
| 54–60s | Name the adjustment and close with an honest next step. |

- **Keep:** The week's meaning, not every activity. Use a small summary board if it helps account for the other goals.
- **Cut:** Six complete dailies stitched together, six repeated intros, an unrelated whole-house reset, or lengthy scores before a story.
- **Payoff:** One clear adjustment and the real reset outcome.
- **Optional question:** “What's one thing you're keeping or changing next week?”
- **Continuity:** Label the actual dates covered. If Sunday's reset has not happened at editing time, don't mark it complete; publish later or report only what is already finished.
- **Format:** If the reflection needs more room, let the vertical edit be longer or commission a separate long-form recap. Do not expand it automatically.
- **Research connection:** Maintain a clear promise and story progression; evaluate the result using watch time and audience response rather than a predetermined duration ([YouTube performance guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16559650), [Shorts analytics guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12942217?hl=en-GB&co=YOUTUBE._YTVideoType=shorts)).

## How Claude Code should apply this

Use this document alongside the Workflow Reset prompt. It supplies video-specific editing approaches; it does not reinstate fixed durations or supersede privacy, source-preservation, and approval rules.

```text
Select the matching weekly video type before editing.
Inspect the source footage and transcript; do not infer events from the calendar.
Write the episode's true goal, outcome, and story question in one sentence each.
Use its example timeline as a starting structure, never a compulsory runtime.
Create a source-timecoded edit decision list using the project's existing schema.
For each cut or reorder, explain its effect on clarity, progress, or connection.
Keep meaningful personality and remove repeated information.
If essential context or a result is missing, flag it; never invent a voiceover or outcome.
Render one low-resolution preview and validate captions, audio, framing, and chronology.
Report what changed and why; do not claim retention has improved before audience data exists.
Do not publish, overwrite originals, or change my treatment/training routine.
```

## Evaluate the next two weeks

Repeat each story type when it fits the real week and record results seven days after each approved publication. Compare matching topics, similar lengths, and the same platform; one previous Reel is only a reference, not a universal baseline.

- **Opening:** Review available early-retention or chose-to-watch/skip measures, without treating differently defined platform metrics as interchangeable.
- **Story progression:** Look at where viewers leave relative to the setup, attempt, and payoff.
- **Depth:** Read average watch time alongside runtime and platform-reported percentage viewed where available.
- **Connection:** Note substantive comments, shares, saves, and attributed follows where the platform exposes them.
- **Decision:** Repeat an approach that shows improvement across more than one post; treat mixed results as a reason to test again, not rebuild the whole channel.

YouTube recommends comparing stronger and weaker Shorts and checking watch time, likes, and comments when experimenting, rather than measuring every post against a viral hit ([YouTube Shorts analytics guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12942217?hl=en-GB&co=YOUTUBE._YTVideoType=shorts)). The seven-day checkpoint and two-week schedule here are practical testing choices, not platform rules or a controlled experiment.

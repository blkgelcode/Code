## 1. Goal (how this channel differs from the studio default)

The studio's goal is consistency and craft, not growth (`BRAND_BIBLE.md`
§9). This channel's brief says something extra: **every video has to get
seen.** Packaging gets as much care as the film. Both goals apply here:

- Consistency and craft still come first. A week that ships something
  honest beats a week spent chasing a number.
- Packaging is treated as part of the craft. A strong title and thumbnail
  are how a good video reaches the people it was made for.
- Analytics are used to find problems (§9), not as targets. No sub or
  watch-hour goals.

## 2. Positioning `[PROPOSED]`

**Five-second version:** *A real beginner learning filmmaking in public,
one lesson at a time.*

**The name is the story:** someone who isn't sure they'll make it, trying
anyway, and growing into a swan. Viewers watch the duckling stage happen
now, not after it's over.

**The gap this channel can own:** The big filmmaking channels (Film Riot,
Indy Mogul, Cinecom, StudioBinder and others) teach from the expert's
side. They show the finished result. Almost nobody shows the whole loop
from the learner's side: *here's the lesson, here's me trying it, here's
what actually happened.* The awkward first attempt is the content, not
something to hide.
*(This gap is a judgment from browsing the niche, not measured data.
Re-check it as the channel grows.)*

**Format promise (every Film 101 episode):** Learn it → Try it → Show it.
1. The lesson, explained simply.
2. Her practicing it, on camera, mistakes included.
3. The result side by side: the attempt, then what she'd change.

## 3. Audience `[PROPOSED]`

- **Who:** people new to filmmaking. Usually they have a phone or a cheap
  camera and no film school. They're unsure they'll succeed but willing
  to try.
- **What they want:** permission to be bad at it at first, plus one
  concrete skill per video they can use this weekend.
- **What turns them off:** gear snobbery, jargon without an explanation,
  and "10 years of experience" energy.
- **Per-video rule:** every package names *who it's for* and *the one
  problem or curiosity it serves* before titles get written.

## 4. Tone of voice `[PROPOSED]`

Honest, warm, encouraging, never preachy.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| "I tried this and it looked bad. Here's why." | "Here's the secret pros don't tell you." |
| Name the specific thing (shot size, f-stop, the 180° rule) | Vague vibes ("level up your cinematic game") |
| Explain any term the first time it's used | Assume the viewer knows the jargon |
| Celebrate being a beginner | Apologize for being a beginner |
| "Try it with me" | "You need to do this" |

## 5. Naming pattern — Film 101 `[PROPOSED]`

**Format:** `<Hook title, strongest words first> | Film 101 Ep N`

- The hook comes first and must work alone for a stranger. Phones cut
  titles off at roughly 40–50 characters, so the series tag at the end is
  allowed to be cut off.
- The series identity also shows up in the thumbnail tab (§6), the
  playlist, and the description's first line, so it never depends on the
  title alone.
- Keep the whole title at 60 characters or fewer when possible. The hook
  should be 45 or fewer.
- Example: `I Shot Every Shot Size in One Day | Film 101 Ep 3`

**Bonus videos** (not Film 101 lessons) carry no Film 101 tag. Use:
- `Homework Reveal:` for her finished practice piece of a lesson
- `Behind the Scenes:` for her process, gear, failures, and setups

## 6. Thumbnail system `[PROPOSED]`

**Rules (proven practice):** design for the phone first. One clear subject,
high contrast, 3 words or fewer, and a readable face or moment. Use real
frames from her footage only, never AI images (studio non-negotiable).
Make two concepts per video for testing.

**Palette** `[LOCKED 2026-11-10]` (ink, cream, bright lemon yellow, and
the teal-blue, so it feels like a notebook):

| Role | Name | Hex |
|---|---|---|
| Text, outlines | Pond Ink | `#141414` |
| Text panel, background block | Swan Cream | `#F7F3E8` |
| Accent: series tab, one highlighted word | Duckling Yellow | `#FFD23F` |
| Second accent, the blue (sparingly) | Pond Teal | `#1F6F78` |

Duckling Yellow is a brighter lemon than @blkgrlcotoo's marigold, so the
two brands don't blur together. She confirmed the yellow and the blue on
2026-11-10.

**Fonts** (from her unassigned library, `../FONT_LIBRARY.md`):
- Headline: **Seriously Nostalgic, Condensed Bold** (or the boldest
  condensed cut). It's chunky enough to read at phone size.
- Annotations: **Hello Scratchy** (Solid + Doodles layers). Use it for
  hand-drawn arrows, circles, and "✗ / ✓" marks on her attempt frames.
  It looks like a student's notebook, which is the brand.
- Max two fonts per thumbnail. Never use the other brands' fonts
  (Advercase, TAN Memories, Eighties Comeback).

**Layout:**
- **Face or moment** fills one side (about 55%). Her real reaction:
  concentration, surprise, a wince at a bad take.
- **Proof** goes on the other side: the frame she shot, a before/after
  pair, or the gear/setup in question.
- **Text:** 1–3 words in Pond Ink on a Swan Cream block, or in cream with
  a heavy ink outline over dark footage. One word can be Duckling Yellow.
- **Series tab:** a small yellow rounded tag, top-left, reading
  `FILM 101 · 03`. Same size and position on every episode.
- **Logo:** a small duckling mark, top-right, or none. **Never
  bottom-right**, because YouTube's duration stamp covers that corner.
- **Hello Scratchy marks:** at most one per thumbnail.

**A/B testing:** YouTube Studio's built-in A/B test (Test & Compare) lets
her test up to 3 titles, 3 thumbnails, or combinations. It needs Advanced
Features enabled on desktop. Use clearly different concepts, because
near-identical versions often end with no winner. Check in Studio which
metric it picks the winner by before reading too much into a result.

## 10. Analytics diagnosis order

Check problems in this order and fix one or two things at a time:
1. **Low impressions** → the topic or search is the problem
   (keywords, topic demand).
2. **Low click-through rate (CTR) with decent impressions** → the title
   and thumbnail are the problem. A title or thumbnail swap within the
   first 48 hours is justified when CTR is clearly below the channel's
   own early baseline.
3. **Low retention** → the video itself is the problem (the hook, pacing,
   or a broken promise).

Early numbers on a new channel are noisy. Judge trends across 3–4
episodes, not one upload.

# Claude Code Editor Checklist

One checklist for every @blkgrlco edit. Her own version, received
2026-11-13 (verbatim: `../brand-bible/source/blkgrlco-claude-code-editor-checklist.md`),
built from the Nov 11 workflow reset plus retention, motion and
shot-variety tips; revised the same day with a Shoot day section and her Story and
growth rules. Run it top to bottom. Story-type guidance per video
lives in `../brand-bible/brands/blkgrlco.md` → HHH Weekly Edit Breakdown.

## 0. Story and growth (her rules, 2026-11-13)

Verbatim source: `../brand-bible/source/blkgrlco-story-and-growth-checklist.md`.

- [ ] Keep every episode connected to her 90-day Hot, Happy, Healthy
      challenge.
- [ ] Identify one reason a new viewer would care: a relatable
      situation, useful takeaway, or real question.
- [ ] When activities repeat, vary the story angle, not her identity or
      routine. Never invent drama, dialogue, or results.
- [ ] Prioritize a clear opening, understandable progression, her
      personality, and an honest payoff before adding effects.
- [ ] Keep production manageable. Do not add extra posts, footage
      requirements, or complicated edits without a clear purpose.
- [ ] Use actual audience feedback to suggest one specific experiment at
      a time. Distinguish observations from assumptions.
- [ ] Do not overhaul the strategy after one low-performing post or treat
      low views as a judgment of her personality. Look for patterns
      across comparable posts.

## Shoot day (new)

- [ ] Coverage: at least 5 shot types per scene: wide to establish,
      medium for action and talking, close-up for emotion, an insert for
      a specific detail, and one creative shot.
- [ ] Mechanical moments (walking in, pouring, setting things down):
      shoot at least 3 takes at different energy levels.
- [ ] Reactions and the honest result: one real take, no redo.
- [ ] Mix a few great b-roll shots with simple handheld shots of her
      talking through the struggle, so the contrast holds attention.
- [ ] Not every frame has her in it. Use objects and the environment, and
      stay behind the camera sometimes.
- [ ] On a tripod, move something in the frame (her body or an object)
      instead of the camera.
- [ ] Write down every "wouldn't it be cool" idea, then shrink it to what
      she can film alone.

## 1. Before the build

- [ ] Read all footage and write the master footage doc
      (`_footage-master.md`, as BLKGRLCO Bin writes it).
- [ ] State the one story in one line: question → context → attempt →
      honest result → takeaway.
- [ ] Write the hook (a real number or thing, said in the first 5
      seconds) and check it matches the title and thumbnail promise.
- [ ] Apply the D-Log M to Rec.709 LUT, then reframe with her face
      centered.

## 2. Edit list (sent before any render)

- [ ] Every clip gets a one-line reason it's there, plus what changed from
      the clip before. If nothing changed, cut it.
- [ ] Simplify: remove context the viewer doesn't need.
- [ ] B-roll must show what she's saying. Remove decorative shots.
- [ ] Pop-ups and on-screen text land on the exact word, never early. If
      early, wait about 2 seconds.
- [ ] Shot variety from real footage: wide to establish, tight detail,
      low angle, close-up on a reaction.
- [ ] Punch-ins only on real reactions, with a hold (identical keyframes)
      before the move.
- [ ] Cut dead air in talking-heads. Keep a pause only when it carries
      the moment.
- [ ] The honest result, including a missed goal, gets its full time.
      Don't rush the payoff.
- [ ] The takeaway answers the question the video opened with.

## 3. Motion specs

- [ ] Pop-ups: scale 0 → 105% → 100%, opacity 0 → 100, and position
      sliding in from the edge, over 8–10 frames.
- [ ] Titles: slide in from off-screen with ease-in on the end keyframe.
      Optional opacity fade 0 → 100.
- [ ] PNG titles go on the track above footage, keyframed in the same way.

## 4. Low-res preview review

- [ ] First 5 seconds confirm the title and thumbnail promise.
- [ ] Every section has something the viewer is waiting for.
- [ ] No section repeats an idea already shown.
- [ ] Judged on curiosity, cut, clarity and connection. Send only the
      best version.

## 5. House rules

- [ ] No employer or job named or shown.
- [ ] Phone on screen only when essential.
- [ ] Skincare: product names are shown, never blurred or cropped out
      (her call, 2026-11-13). No sound effects.
- [ ] YouTube uses original stickers only. TikTok/IG stickers come from
      her cue list.
- [ ] Poppins for @blkgrlco only: captions and on-screen text, nothing
      else (confirmed 2026-11-13).

## 6. Before posting (watch time and thumbnails are the priority through Dec 22)

- [ ] She signs off.
- [ ] Every long-form has three thumbnails (real photos, never
      AI-looking) and three titles.
- [ ] Every long-form is cut into a Short that starts mid-action.
- [ ] Posted at 3:00 PM CT, in numerical order.

## 7. Platform packaging (her checklist, 2026-11-13)

Verbatim source: `../brand-bible/source/blkgrlco-platform-packaging-checklist.md`.
Per-platform detail and the per-episode table: `../growth/SEO_GUIDE.md`.
The full operating prompt for a packaging task, with the inspect-first
steps, pickup-line rule, crop check and deliverable format:
`PLATFORM_PACKAGING_PROMPT.md`.

**Core story**
- [ ] Keep the video connected to her 90-day Hot, Happy, Healthy
      challenge.
- [ ] Identify the intended viewer, specific situation, and honest payoff.
- [ ] Lead with the episode's story; keep "Day [verified number]/90"
      secondary.
- [ ] Preserve her personality, privacy, and actual experience. Never
      invent results.

**YouTube long-form, only when requested**
- [ ] Write an accurate title built around a clear question, task, or
      outcome.
- [ ] Suggest a complementary thumbnail that communicates one main idea.
- [ ] Make the opening deliver the title/thumbnail promise without a long
      introduction.

**YouTube Shorts**
- [ ] Open with relevant action or a clear spoken premise, not a static
      cover.
- [ ] Write a standalone title and select a representative thumbnail
      frame.
- [ ] Resolve the opening question without padding or a forced runtime.

**TikTok**
- [ ] Choose a relatable situation or specific search intent.
- [ ] Use natural topic language in the opening, on-screen text, and
      caption.
- [ ] Label search phrases "unvalidated" unless actual search data
      supports them.
- [ ] Use a clear cover label; include trends or hashtags only when
      relevant.

**Instagram Reels**
- [ ] Open with a recognizable situation, feeling, or useful action.
- [ ] Create a readable cover and a caption combining the topic with
      personal meaning.
- [ ] Give viewers a genuine reason to relate, save, or share. Avoid
      engagement bait.
- [ ] Export from a clean master without another platform's visible
      watermark.

**Production and testing**
- [ ] Reuse the core edit. Generate only the platform versions she
      requests.
- [ ] Avoid invented algorithm weights, hashtag quotas, and universal
      duration targets.
- [ ] Choose one packaging element to test within each platform.
- [ ] Compare similar posts at the same age; keep platform metrics
      separate.
- [ ] Do not claim improved performance until actual audience data
      supports it.

**Return for each requested platform**
- Title or caption
- Cover text, thumbnail concept, or selected frame
- Opening line and on-screen text
- Intended viewer and story promise
- One test hypothesis and metrics to review

Do not publish, schedule, or overwrite originals without authorization.
